[PUBLICATION 01]
Title: Where Does AI Go from Here?
Authors: James Lee; Benjamin Yeo
Journal: Communications of the Association for Information Systems
Volume: 57
Status in CV: In press
Summary:
The study looks at AI's historical development, its current landscape and possible future direction.
It uses natural-language-processing sentiment analysis to examine public perceptions of AI and
discusses challenges and risks. The paper develops a framework for thinking about a more symbiotic
relationship between AI systems and humans rather than treating technological progress as a purely
technical trajectory.
Source link: https://aisel.aisnet.org/cais/vol57/iss1/70/
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 02]
Title: Alternate View for Investigating Data Science Knowledge Gaps: A U.S. Perspective
Authors: Delvin Grant; Benjamin Yeo; Binu Jacob
Journal: Information Development
DOI: 10.1177/02666669241307022
Summary:
The paper compares language used in U.S. university data-science syllabi with language appearing in
industry job advertisements. Text-mining results suggest that university programmes provide
relatively strong preparation in some softer or broader skills while gaps remain in some technical
and analytical requirements sought by employers. The study frames the issue as an industry-
academia alignment problem rather than assuming that either side provides a complete definition of
data-science knowledge.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 03]
Title: The Technology Paradox Explained in Stages and Technology Presence
Authors: Benjamin Yeo; Delvin Grant
Year: 2024
Journal: International Journal of Business and Economics
Volume/Issue: 23(1)
Pages: 87–112
Summary:
Using U.S. regional economic data, the study examines why technology investment does not produce
the same performance effect in every setting. Regions are considered at different stages of growth
and technology presence. The results indicate that areas with stronger technology presence are
better able to leverage technology investment, while the effects vary with stage of development.
The paper therefore treats the productivity/technology paradox as context-dependent rather than as a
single universal relationship.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 04]
Title: How Virtual Mirrors in Advertising Influence Attitudes about Beauty Products
Authors: Hui-Fei Lin; Benjamin Yeo; Tsai-Yi Lu
Year: 2024
Journal: Journal of Advertising Research
Volume/Issue: 64(3)
Pages: 372–392
DOI: 10.2501/JAR-2024-016
Summary:
The study examines augmented-reality virtual mirrors used in beauty-product advertising. It
considers self-view versus other-view presentation, endorsement, beauty filters and user
characteristics such as narcissism/self-presentation. The findings indicate that virtual-mirror
design can improve service and product attitudes through mediating mechanisms, although stronger
attitudes do not automatically translate into purchase intention. The work highlights how AR
features and consumer psychology interact.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 05]
Title: Consumer-Generated Visual Advertisements in Social Media Brand Communities
Authors: Hui-Fei Lin; Pei-Chih Lin; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2024
Journal: International Journal of Communication
Volume: 18
Summary:
The study develops and tests a model of consumer-generated visual advertising within social-media
brand communities. It links users' motivations and perceived value to brand attitudes, community
identification, engagement, word-of-mouth and purchase-related outcomes. The results show that the
effects of consumer-generated advertising are tied to both the value users derive from participation
and their relationship with the brand community.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 06]
Title: Effective Digital Advertising: The Influence of Customized Ads, Self-Esteem, and Product Attributes
Authors: Evelyn Linardi; Hui-Fei Lin; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2024
Journal: Journal of Creative Communications
Volume/Issue: 19(2)
Pages: 197–216
Summary:
A 2x2x2 experiment with 240 Taiwanese participants aged 16–34 examines customised versus
non-customised advertising, utilitarian versus hedonic products, and high versus low self-esteem.
Participants with higher self-esteem respond more favourably to customised advertising, while
customisation has a different relationship with purchase intention among lower-self-esteem
participants. Hedonic product attributes also matter, particularly under non-customised advertising.
The paper shows that personalisation is not uniformly effective across audiences or product types.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 07]
Title: Augmented Reality Advertising in Entertainment Programming: An Exploration Across Cultures
Authors: Hui-Fei Lin; Hsin-yi Sandy Tsai; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2023
Journal: Journal of Creative Communications
Volume/Issue: 18(1)
Pages: 40–60
Summary:
Using a cross-cultural factorial experiment involving U.S. and Taiwanese participants, the study
tests augmented-reality advertising embedded in entertainment programming. Effects vary by culture,
advertising congruence and presentation characteristics. U.S. participants show stronger recall for
some incongruent executions, while Taiwanese participants respond more positively to some congruent,
dynamic AR sponsorship formats. The study argues that AR advertising strategy should be adapted to
cultural context rather than assumed to work identically across markets.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 08]
Title: Predicting Abnormal Trading Behavior from Internet Rumor Propagation: A Machine Learning Approach
Authors: Li-Chen Cheng; Wei-Ting Lu; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2023
Journal: Financial Innovation
Volume/Issue: 9(3)
DOI: 10.1186/s40854-022-00423-9
Summary:
The paper investigates whether patterns in internet-rumour propagation can help predict abnormal
trading behaviour. It compares machine-learning decision-tree models using rumour-related
information with more traditional financial/fundamental information. The results indicate that
rumour-propagation variables can provide stronger predictive information about abnormal trading in
the studied setting, demonstrating the value of online behavioural data for financial-market
analysis.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 09]
Title: Exploratory Study of Societal Contexts and Industry Performance
Authors: Delvin Grant; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2022
Journal: International Journal of E-Business Research
Volume/Issue: 18(1)
Summary:
The study uses an influence-impact framework and data-mining analysis to investigate how technology
and broader societal conditions relate to industry performance. The findings suggest that ICT is
important but not sufficient on its own: technical advancement, financing and geographical/contextual
conditions also shape performance, and their importance changes across stages of development.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 10]
Title: One Size Does Not Fit All: Global Perspectives on IT Worker Turnover
Authors: Benjamin Yeo; Alexander Serenko; Prashant Palvia
Journal: IEEE Transactions on Engineering Management
Volume: 71
Pages: 2843–2852
DOI: 10.1109/TEM.2022.3196585
Summary:
Using survey data from more than 10,000 IT workers across 37 countries, the study examines whether a
turnover model developed in the United States transfers effectively to other national settings.
Decision-tree results show that the U.S. model's applicability declines as cultural, political and
labour-market conditions differ from the U.S.; simple geographical distance is not the key issue.
The paper cautions managers and researchers against treating U.S.-centred IT-workforce models as
universally portable.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 11]
Title: A Business Process Reengineering Method
Authors: Delvin Grant; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2022
Journal: Issues in Information Systems
Volume/Issue: 23(1)
Pages: 1–12
DOI: 10.48009/1_iis_2022_101
Summary:
The paper presents a practical ten-step business-process-reengineering method using UML-based
modelling. It moves from identifying dissatisfaction and analysing the value chain through modelling
the current and redesigned processes. The aim is to give organisations a more structured and
disciplined way to manage process redesign and reduce the risks associated with loosely specified
reengineering efforts.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 12]
Title: Resource-Based View of the Productivity Paradox
Authors: Delvin Grant; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2022
Journal: Technology Analysis & Strategic Management
Volume/Issue: 36(3)
Pages: 533–548
DOI: 10.1080/09537325.2022.2042509
Summary:
Grounded in the resource-based view, the study tests whether technology capability changes the way
technology investment, infrastructure and human capital affect sales growth. Firms/contexts are
clustered into different levels of technology capability and then analysed comparatively. The
results show that the value of particular resources changes with capability level, helping explain
why technology spending alone does not consistently produce productivity gains.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 13]
Title: Impact of High-Tech Industries Over Time
Author: Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2021
Journal: International Journal of Sociotechnology and Knowledge Development
Volume/Issue: 14(1)
Summary:
Using time-series data for U.S. metropolitan areas, the study examines the changing economic
importance of high-tech industries. The effect becomes more visible around the turn of the
millennium, rises and later shows signs of decline. The paper emphasises that the economic role of
high-tech industries is dynamic and has implications for theory, regional policy and development
strategy.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 14]
Title: Enterprise Integration Using Service-Oriented Architecture
Authors: Delvin Grant; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2021
Journal: Issues in Information Systems
Volume/Issue: 22(1)
Pages: 164–177
Summary:
The paper addresses enterprise integration through service-oriented architecture (SOA). Using a
design-oriented approach, it develops a structured method spanning multiple levels of integration
from system specification through broader organisational/global concerns. The proposed framework
includes components such as business-function systems and business-support services and is intended
to make enterprise integration more systematic.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 15]
Title: Job Satisfaction of IT Workers in East Asia: The Role of Employee Demographics, Job Demographics, and Uncertainty Avoidance
Authors: Benjamin Yeo; Alexander Serenko; Prashant Palvia; Osam Sato; Hiroshi Sasaki; Jie Yu; Yue Guo
Year: 2021
Journal: The DATA BASE for Advances in Information Systems
Volume/Issue: 52(2)
Pages: 94–126
Summary:
The study uses decision-tree analysis to examine IT-worker job satisfaction in Taiwan, Japan and
China. Some relationships are shared across settings—for example, greater experience tends to raise
satisfaction and stronger uncertainty avoidance tends to lower it—while other patterns are country
specific. Education does not emerge as a general driver. The work reinforces the importance of
national and cultural context when analysing IT-workforce outcomes.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 16]
Title: The Influence of Social TV Multitasking Behavior on the Effectiveness of Cross-Media Advertising
Authors: Cheng-Hsuan Lin; Hui-Fei Lin; Benjamin Yeo; Pei-Chih Lin
Year: 2021
Journal: International Journal of Communication
Volume: 15
Pages: 1570–1594
Summary:
A 2x2 experiment examines advertising effectiveness when viewers engage in social-TV multitasking.
The results suggest that video advertising can perform better in social-TV/second-screen contexts,
whereas picture-based advertising can be more effective during individual television viewing.
Media gratification partially explains these effects. The study shows that cross-media advertising
effectiveness depends on the viewing environment and ad format.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 17]
Title: Time-Varying Relations Between Seven Dimensions of CSR and Firm Risk
Authors: Wenling Lu; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2020
Journal: Business and Professional Ethics Journal
Volume/Issue: 39(3)
Pages: 319–345
Summary:
Using U.S. data from 1991–2015, the study evaluates how seven dimensions of corporate social
responsibility relate to total firm risk over time. The direction and strength of the relationships
differ across CSR dimensions and market periods. The findings argue against treating CSR as a single
uniform construct and suggest that firms may need to prioritise different CSR activities under
different market conditions.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 18]
Title: What Drives University Technological Innovation Outcomes? A Re-Vitalised Investigation
Author: Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2019
Journal: Journal of Information and Knowledge Management
Volume/Issue: 18(3)
Summary:
Using a technology-organisation-environment perspective, the study re-examines the drivers of
university technological innovation. It considers university-level and regional R&D factors and
their current and future relationships with innovation outcomes. Results support positive effects
for multiple inputs while showing that their future influence differs, reinforcing the need to
consider both institutional and regional context.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 19]
Title: Are ICTs Really That Important in Driving Industry Performance?
Authors: Delvin Grant; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2019
Journal: Journal of Global Information Management
Volume/Issue: 27(3)
Pages: 101–119
Summary:
The study uses decision-tree analysis to compare ICT and financial factors as predictors of industry
performance. Financial conditions—particularly access to bank loans and credit—emerge as stronger
predictors than ICT measures in the analysed data. The paper argues that technology is relevant but
should not be treated as an isolated or automatically dominant driver of business performance.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 20]
Title: Exploring the Factors Affecting Global Manufacturing Performance
Authors: Benjamin Yeo; Delvin Grant
Year: 2019
Journal: Information Technology for Development
Volume/Issue: 25(1)
Pages: 92–121
Summary:
Using decision-tree analysis, the study examines factors associated with manufacturing sales growth
across countries and periods. High ICT environments perform strongly in an earlier period but the
relationship weakens later, while financing variables become more important. The findings show that
ICT alone does not consistently explain manufacturing performance and that financial context and
stage of development matter.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 21]
Title: Exploring the Effects of ICTs, Workforce, and Gender on Capacity Utilization
Authors: Benjamin Yeo; Delvin Grant
Year: 2019
Journal: Information Technology for Development
Volume/Issue: 25(1)
Pages: 122–150
Summary:
The study examines how technology, organisational/workforce characteristics and gender-related
workforce composition relate to capacity utilisation. Regression analysis tests multiple hypotheses
covering factors such as workforce stability and skills, certification, training and female
participation. Results demonstrate that capacity utilisation is shaped by a combination of
technological and human/organisational conditions.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 22]
Title: An Exploratory Perspective on How Crime and Informality Affect BRICS Industry Performance
Authors: Delvin Grant; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2018
Journal: Journal of Global Information Technology Management
Volume/Issue: 21(4)
Pages: 242–261
Summary:
The paper studies industry performance in the BRICS economies through a technology-organisation-
environment perspective, focusing on crime and informal-sector conditions. Regression results show
that factors such as years operating informally, formal registration and perceptions of crime,
theft or disorder can relate to performance. ICT may help offset some constraints, but it does not
erase the importance of the surrounding institutional environment.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 23]
Title: A Global Perspective on Tech Investment, Financing, and ICT on Manufacturing and Service Industry Performance
Authors: Delvin Grant; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2018
Journal: International Journal of Information Management
Volume: 43
Pages: 130–145
Summary:
Using clustering and decision-tree analysis with global industry data, the study examines how
technology investment, financing and ICT relate to performance across different stages of
technology capability. Lower-technology groups depend more on loans and technology investment;
during transition, technology investment becomes especially important; and in higher-technology
settings, bank-lending conditions become more prominent. The effects therefore vary with
technology maturity.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 24]
Title: Societal Impact of University Innovation
Author: Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2018
Journal: Management Research Review
Volume/Issue: 41(11)
Pages: 1309–1335
Summary:
Using patent and regional-economic data, the study examines whether university innovation produces
broader societal/economic effects. Regression results indicate a sustained relationship, but
innovation alone is not sufficient to explain regional outcomes and different innovation measures
do not behave identically. The study argues for attention to context and to the particular way
university innovation is measured.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 25]
Title: Predicting Service Industry Performance Using Decision Tree Analysis
Authors: Benjamin Yeo; Delvin Grant
Year: 2018
Journal: International Journal of Information Management
Volume/Issue: 38(1)
Pages: 288–300
Summary:
Using World Bank service-industry data and a technology-organisation-environment framing, the study
uses decision trees to identify predictors of performance. Financial variables, particularly loans
and credit, are more powerful predictors than ICT measures in the analysed data. Some ICT measures,
such as website presence in particular regional contexts, do not produce the expected positive
relationship. The paper cautions against assuming that ICT by itself drives service-industry growth.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 26]
Title: Mobilizing Location-Based Advertising: A Study of Effectiveness on Persuasion
Authors: Hui-Fei Lin; Benjamin Yeo; Yen-Shen Chen
Year: 2013
Journal: Australian Journal of Business and Management Research
Volume/Issue: 3(6)
Pages: 15–29
Summary:
A 2x2x2 experiment studies mobile advertising format, location-based service and timeliness.
Multimedia messaging combined with location and timely delivery improves several persuasion
outcomes, including attention, interest, desire, brand attitude and purchase intention, although
consumer irritation can mediate these effects. The study shows that mobile advertising
effectiveness depends on execution and context rather than simply on sending a location-aware ad.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 27]
Title: Strategies for Effective Product Placements in Taiwanese Idol Dramas
Authors: Pin-Chun Chen; Hui-Fei Lin; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2013
Journal: American Journal of Business and Management
Volume/Issue: 2(4)
Pages: 357–369
Summary:
Combining experimental evidence with expert interviews, the study examines how product-placement
exposure and connection to a programme's plot affect consumer responses in Taiwanese idol dramas.
Higher exposure combined with stronger plot integration improves brand attitudes. The paper
develops practical product-placement strategies that depend on how visibly and meaningfully the
brand is incorporated into the programme.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 28]
Title: Driving the Knowledge Economy: The Impact of Regional Innovation Capacity
Author: Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2010
Journal: Contemporary Management Research
Volume/Issue: 6(1)
Pages: 73–88
Summary:
Using U.S. metropolitan data from 1988–2007, the study examines the relationship between regional
innovation capacity and economic performance. The influence of innovation becomes stronger after
the dot-com era but remains relatively modest and varies across regional industrial/contextual
conditions. The paper argues for a more focused, context-sensitive interpretation of innovation-led
regional growth.
[/PUBLICATION]

[PUBLICATION 29]
Title used by publisher/index: Environmental Influences on Gender in the IT Workforce
CV wording: The Interaction Between Gender and Environmental Context: Influences on Women in the IT Workforce
Authors: Eileen Trauth; Jeria Quesenberry; Benjamin Yeo
Year: 2008
Journal: The DATA BASE for Advances in Information Systems
Volume/Issue: 39(1)
Pages: 8–32
DOI: 10.1145/1341971.1341975
Summary:
Drawing on a multi-year field study of women working in information technology in multiple U.S.
regions, the paper examines how socio-cultural and environmental factors can operate as barriers or
facilitators. The findings do not support a single universal set of environmental influences:
regional conditions differ and individuals respond differently to those conditions. The study
therefore treats gender and IT-workforce participation as situated phenomena.

[PUBLICATION 30 — PROFESSIONAL-SITE RECORD]
Title: Exploring the Situated Context of Knowledge Management in E-Government Development
Authors: Haiyan Huang; Benjamin Yeo; Eileen Trauth
Year: 2006
Journal: International Journal of Advanced Media and Communications
Summary:
The paper examines the difficulty of transferring private-sector knowledge-management ideas
directly into public-sector/e-government settings. Using the San Joaquin Valley as a contextual
case, it argues for a situated approach that identifies social and institutional conditions shaping
knowledge-management practice. The central point is that e-government knowledge management must be
understood within its local societal context.
Source note:

Volume/issue metadata differs between the professional site and publisher records, so the knowledge
base deliberately does not resolve that discrepancy without further confirmation.
