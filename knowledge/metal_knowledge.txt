DCLXVI — GENERAL METAL MUSIC KNOWLEDGE

Purpose
-------
This file gives DCLXVI enough explicit metal vocabulary and history to discuss metal naturally.
It is GENERAL KNOWLEDGE. Nothing here proves that Benjamin Yeo personally likes a band, album,
genre or technique. Personal preferences must come from music.txt or later user-supplied material.

Heavy metal: broad outline
--------------------------
Heavy metal developed from late-1960s and early-1970s heavy rock. Black Sabbath is central to the
genre's early codification; Judas Priest helped sharpen the style and image; Motörhead connected
heavy metal and punk energy; and the New Wave of British Heavy Metal (NWOBHM), including Iron
Maiden and Saxon, helped shape the faster and more melodic forms that followed.

Genre labels are useful but imperfect. Bands often cross boundaries, scenes evolve, and fans may
disagree about classification. DCLXVI should explain distinctions without pretending the taxonomy
is a natural law. Metal has enough arguments already.

Major styles
------------
Traditional / classic heavy metal:
- Riff-driven heavy music rooted in the 1970s and early 1980s.
- Often features melodic guitar leads, prominent vocals and conventional song structures.
- Examples commonly associated with the style include Black Sabbath, Judas Priest and Iron Maiden.

Thrash metal:
- Faster, more aggressive development influenced by NWOBHM and hardcore punk.
- Common features include rapid alternate picking, palm-muted riffs and energetic drumming.
- Metallica, Slayer, Megadeth and Anthrax are the best-known U.S. "Big Four", though thrash scenes
  extend far beyond them.

Death metal:
- Typically uses heavily distorted guitars, death-growled vocals, aggressive drumming and complex
  or extreme riffing.
- Important early/defining bands include Death, Morbid Angel and Obituary; Cannibal Corpse is a
  major later/continuing example.
- Technical, melodic, brutal and progressive death metal are among its many branches.

Black metal:
- Common traits include tremolo-picked guitar, blast-beat drumming, harsh vocals and often a raw or
  atmospheric production aesthetic.
- The Norwegian early-1990s scene is historically influential, but black metal is geographically
  and stylistically much broader.
- Examples commonly discussed in genre history include Mayhem, Darkthrone and Emperor.
- Avoid reducing the entire genre to controversies associated with a subset of artists.

Doom metal:
- Emphasises slower tempos, weight, atmosphere and sustained riffs.
- Draws heavily from the darker and slower side of Black Sabbath.
- Traditional doom, funeral doom, death-doom, sludge and stoner-doom overlap in different ways.

Power metal:
- Often melodic, fast and harmonically bright, with clean vocals, prominent leads and sometimes
  fantasy-oriented themes.
- Helloween and Blind Guardian are important examples in the European tradition.

Progressive metal:
- Uses structural complexity, changing metres, extended forms and/or technically demanding
  musicianship.
- Dream Theater is a major example; other progressive-metal approaches can sound very different.

Groove metal:
- Focuses on rhythmic weight and syncopated mid-tempo riffing rather than constant speed.
- Pantera and Machine Head are commonly associated with the style.

Industrial metal:
- Combines metal with industrial/electronic textures, programmed elements or machine-like rhythms.
- The balance between metal and industrial influences varies considerably between artists.

Metalcore:
- Combines metal riffing with hardcore-punk elements.
- Breakdowns are common, although metalcore includes several distinct generations and styles.

Deathcore:
- Combines death-metal vocabulary with metalcore/hardcore breakdown structures and extreme vocals.

Melodic death metal:
- Death-metal intensity combined with stronger melodic guitar writing, especially associated with
  the Gothenburg scene.
- At the Gates, In Flames and Dark Tranquillity are major reference points.

Grindcore:
- An extreme style drawing from hardcore punk and metal, often characterised by very short songs,
  high speed and blast beats.
- Napalm Death is foundational.

Symphonic / folk and other hybrids:
- Symphonic metal incorporates orchestral/symphonic textures.
- Folk metal incorporates folk instrumentation, melodic traditions or themes.
- Many bands mix these with black, death, power or other metal styles.

Drumming vocabulary
-------------------
Blast beat:
A family of very fast patterns alternating or combining snare, kick and cymbal/hi-hat attacks.
There is more than one blast-beat technique.

Double-bass / double-kick:
Use of two bass-drum beaters, usually through two pedals or a double pedal, for sustained fast
patterns or rhythmic figures.

Skank beat / d-beat:
Fast punk-derived patterns that appear in thrash, death, black and related styles.

Breakdown:
A rhythmically emphasised, often slower or more spacious section designed around impact and groove.
Especially common in hardcore-derived metal styles.

Ghost note:
A quieter drum stroke used for articulation and groove.

Linear drumming:
A pattern in which limbs generally avoid striking at the same instant, creating interlocking lines.

Odd metre / changing metre:
Time signatures other than regular 4/4 or frequent shifts between metres. Common in progressive
metal but not exclusive to it.

Trigger:
An electronic sensor used with an acoustic drum, especially kick drums, to produce or reinforce a
consistent sampled signal. Using triggers is a production/performance choice, not evidence that a
drummer is "cheating".

Guitar / arrangement vocabulary
--------------------------------
Riff:
A recurring guitar/bass figure forming a structural or rhythmic identity.

Palm muting:
Muting strings near the bridge with the picking hand to create a tighter, percussive sound.

Tremolo picking:
Rapid repeated picking of a note or chord; especially associated with black metal but widely used.

Down-tuning:
Tuning below standard pitch to alter range, tension and tonal weight.

Breakdown, bridge, solo, intro, outro:
Structural labels whose exact functions vary by style.

Production vocabulary
---------------------
Raw production:
A deliberately or historically less-polished sound. Commonly associated with some black metal and
underground traditions, but "raw" is not synonymous with "bad".

Scooped mids:
An EQ approach reducing midrange, historically associated with some metal guitar tones.

Layering / double-tracking:
Recording multiple performances to thicken guitars, vocals or other parts.

Compression:
Controls dynamic range. Extreme-metal production may use substantial compression, but the amount
and purpose vary.
