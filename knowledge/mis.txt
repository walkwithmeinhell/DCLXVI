DCLXVI — MANAGEMENT INFORMATION SYSTEMS

Purpose
-------
This file grounds DCLXVI in supplied MIS teaching material. It covers introductory information
systems, data/knowledge management, AI in an MIS context and project management.

============================================================
1. WHY INFORMATION SYSTEMS MATTER
============================================================

The informed user
- Business professionals benefit from understanding what is behind the information systems they use.
- Informed users can contribute to selecting and improving organisational IT applications.
- Information systems knowledge helps users understand new technologies and how IT can affect
  organisational performance.

Computer-based information systems (CBIS)
- Core components include hardware, software, databases, networks, procedures and people.
- Information systems collect, process, store and distribute information to support decision making,
  coordination and control.
- A useful distinction is data -> information -> knowledge.
- Input captures raw data; processing transforms data; output delivers information; feedback helps
  evaluate or correct the process.
- A computer or program is a technical tool; an information system includes technology together with
  organisational and human components.

Types of organisational systems
- Functional-area information systems.
- Enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems.
- Transaction processing systems (TPS).
- Interorganisational information systems.
- E-commerce systems.
- Office automation systems.
- Business intelligence systems.
- Expert systems and dashboards.

Managing information resources
- The traditional IS function includes systems development/project management, operations, staffing,
  technical services and infrastructure planning/control.
- More consultative roles include strategic systems, e-commerce, systems integration, outsourcing,
  business partnerships and using combined business/technical knowledge to identify new opportunities.
- Investing in information systems does not guarantee good returns; organisational use and management matter.

IT and organisations/society
- IT can reshape industries, organisational structures and managerial/non-managerial work.
- It can create new jobs and business models while also changing or eliminating some roles.
- Broader effects include employee health/safety, accessibility, healthcare and quality-of-life issues.

2. DATA AND KNOWLEDGE MANAGEMENT IN MIS
============================================================

Traditional file problems
- Separate files can create redundancy, inconsistency, inflexibility, weak security and difficulty
  sharing data across applications.
- Database approaches are intended to reduce these problems and improve integrity and controlled access.

Big data
- The teaching material characterises big data using volume, velocity and variety.
- Sources can include traditional enterprise data, machine/sensor data, social data and images.
- Challenges include untrusted sources, dirty data and rapidly changing data streams.
- Organisations may use big data to identify trends, detect fraud, support experiments,
  micro-segment customers and develop new business models.
- NoSQL and distributed-data approaches may be used when conventional relational systems are not a good fit.

Data warehouses and data marts
- Data warehouses integrate data for analysis and decision support.
- Characteristics include integration, time variation, non-volatility and organisation around business dimensions.
- A warehouse environment includes source systems, integration processes, data storage, metadata,
  data quality/governance and users.
- Online analytical processing (OLAP) supports multidimensional analysis such as slice-and-dice operations.

Data governance and data quality
- Information policy establishes rules, procedures and roles for sharing, standardising and managing data.
- Data governance addresses availability, usability, integrity, security and regulatory responsibilities.
- Data administration and database administration have related but distinct responsibilities.
- Data-quality work includes identifying faulty data, auditing quality and cleansing data.

Knowledge management
- Knowledge-management systems support capturing, organising, sharing and using organisational knowledge.
- Explicit knowledge is easier to articulate and record; tacit knowledge is more personal/experiential
  and can be harder to capture.
- Knowledge management should be treated as a cycle/process rather than simply as a software purchase.

============================================================
3. AI IN AN MIS CONTEXT
============================================================

- AI systems are introduced in relation to capabilities such as learning from experience, handling
  ambiguous information and responding to new situations.
- The material distinguishes broad ideas of strong versus weak AI.
- AI technologies discussed include expert systems, machine learning, deep learning and neural networks.
- Application areas include computer vision, natural-language processing, robotics, image recognition
  and intelligent agents.
- These categories overlap and evolve; they should not be presented as an exhaustive taxonomy.

4. PROJECT MANAGEMENT FOR INFORMATION SYSTEMS
============================================================

Triple constraint
- Time, cost and scope are interrelated constraints in project management.
- Changing one can affect the others; project management therefore involves trade-offs rather than
  maximising each independently.

Project-management process
- Initiation: establish the need, purpose and basic feasibility of a project.
- Planning: define tasks, resources, timing, dependencies, risks and responsibilities.
- Execution: perform the planned project work.
- Monitoring and control: compare actual progress with the plan and take corrective action where needed.
- Completion: formally close the project, deliver outcomes and capture lessons/results.
- Gantt charts are used to visualise project tasks and timing.

Project failure and success
- IS projects can fail because of unclear scope, unrealistic schedules/budgets, weak coordination,
  inadequate risk management, poor communication and other management problems.
- Success requires attention to both technical delivery and organisational/project-management processes.

PMBOK context
- The supplied slides introduce the Project Management Body of Knowledge and its knowledge areas as a
  framework for coordinating project-management responsibilities.
